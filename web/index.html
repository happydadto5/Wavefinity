        Wavefinity Quick Tutorial

        The basic idea is simple: define the physical Space, design the bins that belong in it, then drag those bins into place.

        1. Start with a Space

        A Space is the real area you want to organize. Choose Drawer, Storage Box, Surface, or Pegboard for something you already have — or Storage Drawers to print a new cabinet with drawers.

        The Space keeps its physical size, layout, and inventory together. A Storage Box Space makes its own case, a Surface Space makes its own Base Trim, and a Storage Drawers Space makes its own printable cabinet. Save or print a case or Base Trim from the Space header; a cabinet has Save Cabinet and Print Cabinet beside its drawer list.

        2. Design your bins

        A Design is the bin you are building. Set its width, depth, height, wall and base choices, labels, stacking choices, and other shell options here. It saves as you go.

        Use the top-level Space and Design buttons to change work areas. Design has 3D and 2D views; Space shows the overall layout.

        3. Add Options

        Options customize what the bin holds or how the shell behaves. Interior features include things such as Dividers, Bores, Cradles, Slot Racks, and Photo Nests. Shell options can change labels, grips, openings, lids, stacking, or mounts.

        Add only what the bin needs. The 3D preview shows the combined result as you work.

        4. Arrange things inside a bin

        Switch to 2D when you want to position interior parts. Select a part, then move or arrange it inside the bin footprint. Use 3D again to check clearances and the finished shape.

        Some parts can size themselves from what they hold. If an auto-sized part needs more room, Wavefinity can grow the bin to keep the part valid instead of silently clipping it.

        5. Arrange bins inside the Space

        Every bin you design is one row in the active Space's Inventory. Its number badge is separate from its name. Placed or Unplaced describes its physical location; In Design, Saved, or Printed describes its files. Unplaced bins wait beside the Space. Drag one in to place it; drag a placed bin off the Space to unplace it.

        Click an Inventory row to select it. Use its Edit button to open that bin in Design, or its Delete button to remove it. Select multiple rows with their checkboxes for bulk Delete. Spacers appear below ordinary bins.

        6. Connect neighboring bins

        Connectors lock neighboring Wavefinity bins together. Side connectors join a shared seam and can work with different bin heights. 3-Way and 4-Way corner connectors join equal-height, equal-wall bins around a corner.

        You do not set a separate connector wall thickness. Connector fit follows the wall thickness of the bin being designed.

        7. Save or print

        Give the design a useful name, then save files to your folder or send them to your slicer when that option is available. Wavefinity can produce the bin plus any related printable parts that belong to the design.

        To save or print in bulk, open Space Inventory. With nothing selected, Save All Needed makes missing files and Print All Not Printed sends bins to Bambu Studio. Select rows to limit the action to eligible bins. Required Space connectors are included automatically. After Bambu Studio opens, those bins become Printed. If you print elsewhere, use Mark Printed on the row. When you leave a changed bin that had saved files, choose whether to update those files; its editable Space design is already saved.

        A Storage Drawers cabinet saves its own files too: Save Cabinet writes the base, top, sides, rear, every drawer and any stacking pegs, oriented for your printer. The assembly & print guide linked beside it explains which part is which and how they fit together by hand.

        Then keep going: design another bin and build out the Space until the physical area is organized the way you want.

        A good first workflow

        1. Create the Space and enter its real measurements. For a printable cabinet, choose Storage Drawers, check that it fits your printer, then use Save Cabinet.
        2. Design one bin that fits something you actually want to store.
        3. Add Options, then arrange interior parts in 2D.
        4. Check the result in 3D and save or print it.
        5. Open Space, drag the bin from Unplaced bins into the Space, and repeat.
        6. Use connectors and spacers to finish the overall layout.

        Configure Space

        Measure the clear inside width, depth, and height with the drawer closed. Width is left ↔ right; Depth is front ↔ back. Wavefinity handles its required wall clearance automatically.
