Storage Drawers — assembly & print guide

This one page covers every Storage Drawers cabinet. Wavefinity saves each part as its own 3MF file, already turned into the orientation that fits your printer. Nothing needs screws, glue or tools for standard assembly.

1. Which part is which

2. Assembly order

  1. Base: place the Cabinet Base flat with its rails up.
  2. Sides: slide the bottom dovetail of each side into its base rail from the back, pushing forward until it stops. The sides stand upright and parallel.
  3. Rear: lower the rear panel straight down from above into the vertical grooves at the back of both sides. It should drop in under its own weight, with no pressing. It rests in the pocket in the base.
  4. Drawers: push each drawer in straight from the front (see step 4).
  5. Top: slide the Cabinet Top on from the back toward the front over the two top dovetails. Push firmly: each dovetail bump rides up and settles into its relief with a small click when the Top is fully seated.
  6. Stacking: if you made Stack Pegs, press one into each top socket to stack another cabinet above.

Stacking limits: peg positions are taken from the cabinet's outer size, so the pegs only line up between two cabinets with the same width and depth. The pegs are a 0.08 mm radial press fit — on some printers they may need a light easing to seat.

3. Removing the Top

Take out the top drawer first. Hold the cabinet down and slide the Top backward with a firm push of your hand until it comes free. The small bump at the end of each dovetail gives a little resistance as it leaves its relief; nothing needs to be pressed, bent or pried, and no tool is needed.

4. Drawers

First print: before printing the full set, run a small fit test: in the app, set up a one-drawer cabinet with the same width, depth, Drawer fit, wall/style and printer settings as your planned cabinet — the exported sides come out short to match the single drawer. Print one drawer, the base, and both short sides (or frames), assemble them, and check that the drawer runs straight and clicks its catch on both rails before printing the rest. Changing Drawer fit later changes the base, top, sides (or frames), rear and every drawer — the whole cabinet must be reprinted.

Drawers push in and pull out in a straight line, and stop exactly flush with the cabinet front — they cannot be pushed in behind it. There is a light click as a drawer passes its catch — do not lift the drawer. If it feels stiff, choose a looser Drawer fit in the Drawers section, save the cabinet again and reprint the whole cabinet, not only the drawers. Drawer fit changes the cabinet's outside width and depth, the spacing between drawer levels, and the rail, catch and stop positions, so the base, top, sides (or frames), rear and every drawer all change. Avoid Tight on a first print: at that setting the drawer catch has almost no spare travel, so a slightly oversized print can jam instead of clicking.

Drawer travel: the drawer catch is only a 0.25 mm detent — there is no pull-out stop. A loaded drawer can pull fully out of the cabinet, so support it with your other hand when you pull it out.

5. Printing

  • Load each file as it is. The orientation Wavefinity chose is the one it checked against your printer's build volume, so do not rotate it.
  • Use your normal material profile for a rigid part.
  • Use at least 3 perimeters (walls) and about 15% infill.
  • Every Storage Drawers part is designed to print without supports in the specified orientation (the one Wavefinity exported it in). That is a geometry check of overhang angles and short bridges, not a slicer or physical-print guarantee, so look at your slicer's preview. Pull ledges have a 45° underside; the underside of a small bridge may show minor roughness. If your slicer wants supports, first check that the part has not been rotated.
  • If a part will not fit your printer, open Printer Settings… and check the build volume, or reduce the cabinet size or drawer count.

Plate plan (default cabinet)

A default cabinet is eight large parts; if Stacking is on, it also includes four stack pegs — more than one plate holds on most printers. Use the seven-row template below as a grouping and print-order guide, splitting the files onto plates in plate order. (It is a template, not always exactly seven printed plates: the drawer row repeats as needed, and the stack-peg row only applies when Stacking is on.) Every file is already oriented for your printer, so keep that orientation on each plate — do not rotate parts to fit.
